How long a made-to-order bento cake keeps is a frequent question from both clients and beginner bakers. There's no universal answer: it all depends on the cream, the filling and the storage conditions. Let's break it down.

## Fridge: guidelines by cream type

- **Butter-based cream cheese** — the most stable. Keeps 48–72 hours without losing flavour or shape.
- **Cream cheese on whipping cream** — more delicate and demanding; best eaten within 24–36 hours.
- **Mascarpone** — up to 24 hours, especially with a berry filling.
- **Ganache** — the toughest, 72 hours and more, though chocolate "sets" over time and the texture gets denser.

These are guidelines for a home fridge at +4…+6 °C. In a professional display case the numbers can differ slightly.

## Room temperature

A bento cake is always a chilled dessert. On the table at +20…+24 °C it can stand **no more than 1–2 hours**, and even that applies to butter-based creams. With whipped-cream-based creams in warm weather the filling starts running after 30–40 minutes.

Take the cake out of the fridge **15–20 minutes** before serving — the cream warms to the right temperature and the flavour opens up better.

## Freezing

Not recommended. After thawing, cream cheese and mascarpone split — liquid weeps out and the texture turns grainy. Ganache handles freezing a little better, but finishing decor (fresh berries, fondant) doesn't survive.

If you absolutely have to, freeze **before decorating** — sponge and cream separately. Assemble on the day of serving.

## What to tell the client at hand-off

A short note as a tag or a message:

> Keep the cake refrigerated for up to 48 hours. Take it out 15 minutes before serving. Transport it flat, don't turn it upside down.

These three lines prevent 90% of "the cream ran / the cake sank" complaints. More on packaging in [how to package a bento cake for sale](/en/blog/how-to-package-bento/).

## FAQ

**Can the cake sit in the car in summer?**
No more than 30 minutes, even with AC. Better to use a carrier with ice packs.

**The cake stood 2 hours in the heat — is it still safe to eat?**
If the cream holds its shape and there's no off smell — yes. But don't keep it any longer: after that kind of warmth, its fridge life is cut in half.

**What spoils first — the cream or the sponge?**
The cream. Especially whipped-cream-based or mascarpone. The sponge, soaked and wrapped in cream, stays fresh longer — it doesn't touch the air.
