"The cream won't hold the wall" is the most common complaint from people starting out with bento. Before changing the recipe, work out which of three areas the problem sits in — it is almost never the recipe itself.

## Cause one: temperature

Dairy cream is an emulsion of fat and water. It is stable within a narrow temperature corridor and falls apart on both sides of it: ingredients that are too warm never let the cream come together, ones that have partly frozen split.

Kitchen temperature belongs here too. At +25 °C and above every dairy cream slumps, regardless of recipe, and no technique overrides that — the only thing that changes is how many minutes you have before it goes.

## Cause two: the wrong product

Supermarket curd cheese and professional pastry cream cheese are different products in density and fat. The same goes for cream: vegetable and dairy behave differently, and fat content decides the outcome.

If the cream slumped on ingredients you bought for the first time, changing technique is pointless — go back to the ones it worked on.

## Cause three: the cream doesn't match the filling

The most underrated one. Sharp fruit fillings, caramel and curds release moisture and load the cream differently. A cream that holds a dry nut layer perfectly can slump under a berry coulis — that is not a fault of your hands, it is a mismatched pair of cream and filling.

This is why in working collections the cream is not given on its own but inside a specific cake: the pair is chosen in advance and tested.

## What this means in practice

If the cream slumps consistently, on any recipe — look at temperature and ingredients. If it slumps in one cake only, the problem is the cream-and-filling pair, and the answer is a different cream, not a better technique.

A similar but separate problem is [cream that never thickens at all](/en/blog/cream-not-thickening/).
