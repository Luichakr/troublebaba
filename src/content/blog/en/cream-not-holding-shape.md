"The cream won't hold its shape" is the most common problem for anyone just starting out with bento cakes. Sometimes the recipe is at fault; more often it's the technique. Here's what to check first.

## 1. Warm ingredients

The most frequent cause. Cream cheese, whipping cream, mascarpone — all of it works only straight from the fridge. If the cheese has sat on the counter even 30 minutes, it starts to run.

**What to do:** keep the ingredients refrigerated right up until whipping. Chill the mixer bowl and whisk in the freezer for 15 minutes.

## 2. The wrong cheese

Ordinary supermarket cream cheese is not the professional pastry-grade kind. They differ in density and fat content.

**What to do:** look for cake-grade cheese in specialist baking-supply stores. The packaging often says "for cakes" or "professional".

## 3. Over-whipped

If the cream turns grainy, splits or goes watery — you've over-whipped. The milk protein has broken down and the fat has separated.

**What to do:** whip at medium speed and stop the moment the cream is smooth and dense. Better to under-whip and finish by hand with a spatula than to over-whip.

## 4. Too much icing sugar

Sugar binds moisture, but in excess it makes the cream heavy and too sweet. And here's the paradox — an overly sweet cream holds shape worse, because the fat-to-dry balance is off.

**What to do:** stick to the proportions. If the recipe gives a range, take the lower end and taste.

## 5. A warm kitchen

At +25 °C and above, any dairy cream will run. A bento is a chilled dessert, and it gets assembled in a cool kitchen.

**What to do:** cool the work surface (an ice pack under the parchment). Turn on the AC. Work in the morning before it gets hot.

## 6. The cream wasn't given time to stabilise

Freshly whipped cream cheese hasn't "settled" yet. Start smoothing straight away and the cream slides off the rim.

**What to do:** after whipping, let the cream rest **30–60 minutes in the fridge** in a piping bag. It firms up and becomes manageable.

## The cream has already run — rescue it or start over?

A runny cream is rarely saved. You can try chilling the bowl and ingredients and re-whipping — sometimes it helps. If it has split — start over.

The worst is losing a cake at final assembly. So **always whip 15–20% more cream than you need**. The extra can always go on cupcakes or be eaten with berries.

## The most stable cream

If you're just starting and worried the cream won't work out, go with **butter-based cream cheese**. It's the most heat-stable, forgives mistakes, and holds a bento rim well.

## FAQ

**What if the cream turns grainy?**
That's over-whipping. You can try to rescue it: add 1–2 spoonfuls of cold whipping cream and fold in gently with a spatula. Sometimes it recovers.

**Can I swap the butter for a spread?**
No. Spreads contain vegetable fats — the cream will taste greasy and won't hold shape at +20 °C.

**If the cream won't hold — could the sponge be the problem?**
It could. A sponge that's too wet, underbaked or still warm releases moisture into the cream. The sponge must be fully cooled before assembly — better yet, rested in the fridge for a few hours.
