Most complaints — "the cream ran", "the cake arrived crooked" — are not about the recipe. A bento cake holds its shape well in the fridge and almost always suffers on the road.

## Before you leave

Only take a fully chilled cake out on the road. Cream applied recently is still mobile and is at its most vulnerable to vibration and jolts — how long exactly it needs in the fridge depends on the cream and is stated in the recipe.

Don't take the cake out early "to be ready". The longer it stands at room temperature before you leave, the less margin is left for the journey itself. Ideally: take it out, box it, go.

## Box and board

| Point | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Box width** | exactly the cake's diameter, no play | an oversized box lets the cake travel and knock its wall |
| **Box height** | 2–3 cm above the cake | so the lid never touches the decor |
| **Box base** | rigid, no sag | a soft base deforms under the weight and tilts the cake |
| **Board** | 1–2 cm wider than the cake | the cake is fixed and doesn't slide around |
| **Fixing the board** | dabs of cream or double-sided tape underneath | the board stays put even under hard braking |

A box bought "with room to spare" is a classic beginner's mistake: the spare space is exactly what lets the cake rock. More on boxes in [how to package a bento cake](/en/blog/how-to-package-bento/).

## Summer: heat in the car

A car interior in the sun heats up far faster than it feels — even with the air conditioning on, the air near the floor and seats is warmer than in the vents.

- without extra cooling a cake travels **no more than 30 minutes**, air conditioning included;
- with a cool bag or a carrier with ice packs — **up to 1.5–2 hours**;
- put the cake on the floor behind the front seats, away from windows and direct sun;
- never leave a cake in a parked car in the sun, not even for ten minutes.

If the route is longer than 30 minutes with no cool bag, that is already a risk: warn the client or move the handover to a cooler hour.

## Winter: the swing, not the cold

In winter the danger is the temperature swing rather than the cold itself. A cake that has travelled in freezing air and then comes into a warm flat condenses moisture on its surface — the same effect as a cold glass brought indoors.

- let the cake stand closed in its box for 10–15 minutes at room temperature before opening the lid;
- don't leave it in a car in hard frost — cream that freezes and thaws comes back grainy;
- below −10 °C a short car trip is safer for the cake than a slow walk with the box in hand.

## Courier and taxi: what to say first

If somebody else is carrying the cake, a short spoken instruction prevents most of the trouble:

- hold the box **level**, never on its side, never upside down;
- nothing stacked on top, not even light bags;
- avoid the boot — unpredictable temperature, no cushioning;
- steady the box by hand when braking rather than hoping it stays put.

Write it on the lid in large letters as well: "Fragile. Keep level. Do not turn over." That works even when the spoken version is forgotten.

## Checklist before leaving

1. The cake has fully chilled in the fridge after decorating.
2. It came out no more than 15–20 minutes before departure.
3. The box fits exactly and the board is fixed.
4. In summer, a cool bag with ice packs is in the car.
5. The route has no long stops in sun or frost.
6. The courier — or you — has been told: keep it level, never on its side.

A minute of checking removes most of the reasons a cake arrives in worse condition than it left. Shelf life after handover is in [how long a bento cake keeps](/en/blog/bento-cake-shelf-life/).

## When to postpone the trip altogether

At +32 °C and above with no cool bag, it is more honest to move the handover to a cooler hour than to risk a cake that took hours of work. The same goes for hard frost and a long walk: cream that has frozen and thawed will not come back to its original texture.
