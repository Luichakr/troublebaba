A bento cake is a cold dessert. Everything it does well — the sharp wall, the clean edge, the neat cut — depends on it being cold. Out of the fridge that is a countdown, and it is worth knowing how long it runs.

## How long you actually have

At **+20–24 °C** a cake on butter-based cream holds up for **one to two hours**. On lighter dairy creams and mascarpone the filling starts moving after **30–40 minutes**.

Above +25 °C those numbers shorten sharply, and in direct sun they stop applying at all: the surface of the cake heats much faster than the air temperature suggests.

## Why the cream fails suddenly

Cream based on butter or cheese is an emulsion, stable only in a narrow temperature range. From around +24–26 °C the fat in it begins to soften and the cream loses its spring: the wall rounds off, sharp edges disappear, piping spreads.

It happens gradually, which is why the first 20–30 minutes look fine and the failure then seems abrupt. Humidity makes it worse: in a stuffy room the cake "sweats" even at an acceptable temperature, because moisture from the air condenses on its chilled surface.

## Summer and winter are different problems

**In summer** the danger is not only air temperature but direct sun. A short spell on a windowsill or by a car window heats the cream far faster than a thermometer in the room suggests. Keep the cake in shade and away from warm surfaces.

**In winter** the problem inverts: a cake that has travelled in the cold and comes into a warm room condenses moisture on its surface. Let it stand closed in its box for 10–15 minutes before opening — more on that in [how to transport a bento cake](/en/blog/how-to-transport-bento-cake/).

## Planning the timing of a party

The usual mistake is putting the cake on the table with everything else at the start of the evening. Three hours later it has been warm the whole time.

Plan backwards from the moment the cake is actually cut. Keep it in the fridge until then, bring it out 15–20 minutes before, and let those minutes be its only time in the warm. If there is no fridge at the venue, a cool bag with ice packs buys back most of the window.

## Signs it has been out too long

- the wall has rounded off and lost its edge;
- moisture has appeared on the surface — the cream is weeping;
- the filling has started to move visibly at the cut;
- the smell has changed at all — at that point it is no longer a question of looks.

## What to tell the client

Two numbers, in writing, at handover: keep it in the fridge, and take it out 15–20 minutes before serving. That one line removes most complaints about a melted cake, and it protects you: a client who was warned and left the cake in the sun knows whose call it was.
