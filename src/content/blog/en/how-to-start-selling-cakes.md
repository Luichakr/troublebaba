A friend asks you to bake a cake for her birthday. You say no because you're “not ready yet.” This goes on for eighteen months. During that stretch you could have refined your recipes on 20 clients and grown a social channel to 15,000 followers. Instead — Pinterest.

Familiar? Let's separate what you actually need for a first paid order from what's an invented obstacle.

## What you actually need

**One working recipe.** Not ten — one. The one you've already baked twice and know: it comes out. The second and third you'll pick up after your first orders, when a client asks “can we do it with a different filling?”

**Basic equipment.** An oven, one 16 cm tin, a hand mixer under $30, a scale accurate to 1 gram. Stand mixer, silicone mats with thermometers, convection oven — buy those later, from client money.

**Packaging.** A kraft lunch box, greaseproof liners, ribbon. Buy at any local baking supply or online. Budget — under $25 for a starter kit for 5 orders.

**Costing awareness.** Without it you'll either sell at a loss or panic on the price and lose the client. You take a supermarket receipt, divide by grams, multiply by the recipe's gram weight. That's the base — full details in the PDF collection.

## What you don't need (yet)

- A business license. The first 2-3 orders for friends are gifts, not a business. Formalise when you're consistently doing 4+ cakes per week.
- A dedicated kitchen. A home kitchen works fine, as long as you know the limits and don't accept an order for 30 cupcakes.
- A polished Instagram. Fifty friend-followers is enough for your first clients. Audience grows in parallel, not “first.”
- Expensive imported decorations. Fresh fruit, a chocolate crumb, and clean plating look better than an overloaded fondant scene.

## What to do this week

1. Bake the recipe you plan to sell first. A second time, not “the first time in your life, for a client.”
2. Cost that recipe in grams and dollars. Land on a number: “I'm fine selling it for X.”
3. Post one story: “I'm taking one order for Friday, cake for 4-6 servings, price X.” That's it.

The client will find you. They always do, once you finally state the offer.

## What next

After the first 5 orders you'll understand your pace. How many sponges you can bake on a Sunday. What delivery really costs. What questions the client asks. That's the moment to expand your menu — and that's when buying the 10-recipe PDF collection makes the most sense. It pays for itself on order number two.

But first — that one order for Friday.
