A home baker sets her price at "€15, same as the girl two streets over." Loses money on every cake and can't work out why. Bento is easier to start than a full-size cake business, but the cost math is trickier — the cake is small, the setup time isn't, and it's easy to under-price yourself into a hobby that quietly costs you money.

Here's the full breakdown, with a worked example.

*Prices below are in euros at mid-2026 European supermarket levels. For US or UK figures, the structure holds — swap in your local ingredient prices and keep the same multipliers.*

## What makes up the cost of a bento cake

**1. Ingredients.** The first thing people look at. For an average 12 cm bento, this is roughly **€3 to €6**, and the spread is huge because of the flavour: chocolate and cookie-based ones (Oreo, Snickers) sit at the low end, nut and fruit ones (Pistachio-Raspberry, Raffaello, Ferrero Rocher) at 2–2.5× the low end. Count strictly by the recipe's gram list, not "about a pack of cream cheese."

**2. Packaging.** The line most starters forget. A basic bento kit is:
- Bento box (12×12×8 cm or 14×14×8 cm) — €0.60–€1.50
- Cake board or bottom pad — €0.30
- Ribbon / sticker / branded label — €0.40
- Napkin, disposable spatula — €0.20
- Insert card (storage instructions, thank-you) — €0.10

Total: **€1.60–€2.50 per cake**. Buy boxes in batches of 50 — they cost 2–3× more per piece if you order five at a time.

**3. Your time.** The number everyone underestimates. A first-order bento takes **3–4 hours** including sponge, cooling, cream, assembly, decoration, packaging and clean-up. Even at a modest €10/hour that's **€30–€40 of your time** per cake. This is the single biggest reason the "× 3" multiplier exists — it's not greed, it's covering the hours you already spent.

**4. Utilities.** Oven at 170°C for 40 minutes, mixer, refrigerator time. Roughly €0.30–€0.60 per bento cake. Small individually, but adds up across a month of orders.

**5. Tool amortisation.** Silicone rings, offset spatula, piping bags and tips, thermometer, kitchen scales — around €80–€120 initial outlay, spread across the first 100 cakes = **€0.80–€1.20 per cake**.

**6. Failure buffer.** Your first 5–10 batches will have failures. A collapsed sponge, an over-whipped cream, a caramel that seized. Real cost of one failed batch — €5–€8 of wasted product. Averaging that across 20 successful cakes = **€0.30–€0.50 per cake**.

**Rough total for a starter's 12 cm bento:** €35–€45 including your time. Excluding your time (which is what most starters actually compute): €5–€10.

## Worked example: Oreo bento cake, 12 cm

If you sell this cake for €15, you're paying €25 to make it — the €5 "profit" doesn't exist, it's your own subsidised labour.

## What price should you charge

Two workable formulas, use whichever matches your market:

**Product cost × 3.** In the example above: €9.70 × 3 = **€29** as a public price. Covers time, packaging and margin. This is the starter's rule of thumb.

**Full cost × 1.3.** Once you know your time reliably (5+ orders in): €39.70 × 1.3 = **€52** as the price. This is the "professional" number, harder to sell at first, but it's what a bakery would need to charge to keep the lights on.

For a first-time seller in most European cities, **€28–€35 for a base bento (Oreo, Snickers) and €40–€55 for a premium one (Pistachio-Raspberry, Ferrero Rocher, Raffaello)** is the working range. Below €25 you're losing money; above €60 you need a portfolio and Instagram presence to justify.

## How to figure the price for your city

Local market matters. In Warsaw, mid-range bento prices sit around **80–150 zł**. In Berlin, **€25–€45**. In Rome, **€25–€40**. In smaller cities, subtract 20–30%.

Three checks:
1. Search Instagram: `bento cake [your city]` — look at 5–10 profiles, note their prices. Ignore the top 1–2 (celebrity bakers) and the bottom 1–2 (hobbyists losing money). Middle 5 = your market range.
2. Compare product cost. Milk and eggs in Warsaw ≠ milk and eggs in Kyiv. Your ingredient line depends on where you buy.
3. Ask: "would I buy this cake at my price?" If yes, you're probably too cheap. If you'd hesitate, you're at market.

## Pricing by flavour: don't set one flat rate

Big mistake beginners make: charging €25 for everything. It works for Oreo and Snickers, but for Pistachio-Raspberry (€10+ in pistachio paste alone), you're losing money on every order.

Rough tiers:
- **Basic** (Oreo, Snickers, Cinnabon) — chocolate, cookies, caramel. Lowest ingredient cost.
- **Medium** ([Red Velvet](/en/recipes/red-velvet-bento-cake/), Pina Colada, Poppy-Citrus, Cherry Chocolate) — cheese, coconut, fresh or frozen berries.
- **Premium** (Raffaello, Ferrero Rocher, Pistachio-Raspberry) — nuts, coconut, nut pastes. Highest ingredient cost.

Price tiers should reflect this: base tier €28–€30, medium €32–€35, premium €40–€55.

## Two mistakes that kill margin at the start

**1. Free delivery inside the city.** Sounds friendly, kills your margin. A 20-minute drive across the city is €5–€8 of fuel and parking. Multiply by 3 deliveries a week — €60–€90 a month straight from your profit. Charge for delivery, or make pickup the default and offer delivery as an add-on.

**2. Same-day discounts.** "Take one today for €20 instead of €30." You're training regulars to wait for discounts and hurting your average price. If you have a leftover cake, give it to a friend or eat it — never publish a discount. It anchors expectations that hurt you for the next twelve months.

## How much can you actually earn on bento cakes

At 3 bento cakes a week from home:
- €30 × 3 × 4 weeks = **€360/month gross**
- Minus ingredients + packaging (€10–€15 × 12) = €150
- Minus utilities + amortisation (€2 × 12) = €24
- **Net: €186/month** (excluding your time)

Not a career. But if the goal is "cover ingredient costs of my main hobby and put €200 aside," it works.

To scale past that, you need a proper commercial kitchen certification, a pastry cabinet, and 10+ orders a week — which is a different business entirely. Bento is a great **starter** business, not a great **destination** business.

## What makes the cost calculation reliable

The reason most starters can't price properly is that they don't know their own ingredient quantities. "About a pack of cream cheese" turns into 280 g one time and 350 g the next — and the cost per cake swings by €1.50 without you noticing.
