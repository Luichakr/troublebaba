A home baker sets her price at “$8, same as the neighbour.” Loses money on every cake and can't figure out why. Let's break down what actually goes into the cost of goods.

## What goes into cost

**Ingredients.** The first thing anyone looks at. Roughly $3-6 for a mid-range bento cake serving 4-6 people, depending on flavour — Oreo cheaper, pistachio-raspberry 2-2.5× more.

**Packaging for the client.** Box + greaseproof paper + ribbon + candle — $1.50 to $3 per unit. Cheaper in bulk, but for the first orders you're buying one at a time.

**Utilities.** Electricity, gas, water. Per cake it's $0.30-0.80, but you need to count it — because at month-end it stacks up.

**Time.** The line home bakers most often forget. Three hours from zero to packaged bento cake — that's real money. If your time is worth $8/hour, that's $24 into cost.

**Equipment amortisation.** A tin, whisk, silicone spatula — they last 200-500 cakes. Add another $0.20-0.40 per cake.

## Example: an Oreo bento cake

| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Ingredients (flour, cocoa, cream cheese, cream, Oreos, gelatin, berries) | $5.60 |
| Packaging (box, paper, base, ribbon) | $2.20 |
| Utilities + amortisation | $1.20 |
| Your time (3 hours × $8) | $24.00 |
| **Cost of goods** | **$33.00** |

Selling that cake for $28 means you're subsidising the client from your pocket. Selling at $36 means you make $3 and wonder why baking “isn't worth it.”

## What it should actually cost the client

Home-bakery markup rule: **multiply cost by 1.7-2.5**. In the example above — that's $56-83. Sounds high? Compare to a similar cake in a specialty bakery — and remember, they're using substitutes, you're using real mascarpone and 82% butter.

The client willing to pay $60 for your bento cake, with your photos — exists. The one who wants it for $15 — is not your client. They go to the neighbour who doesn't count her time.

## Two mistakes at the start

1. **Not counting time.** Six months in — burnout and “I'm sick of baking.” It's not the baking — it's the physical fatigue from unpaid hours.
2. **Pricing by market instead of by cost.** “Everyone charges $28, so I will too.” The market is the average of people selling at a loss. Your cost may be different because of neighbourhood, ingredient tier, packaging class.
